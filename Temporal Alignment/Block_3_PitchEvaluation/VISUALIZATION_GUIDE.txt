OVERVIEW
--------
We have created easy-to-understand visual reports for all Movement1 pitch 
evaluations. These plots show pitch accuracy in ways that make sense to 
musicians, using note names (like C4, A4) instead of technical measurements.


WHERE TO FIND THE PLOTS
-----------------------
Location: Data_Pitch/pitch_results_movement1/segment##/pitch_evaluation_*/
          musician_visualizations/

Each segment evaluation contains 4 PNG image files that you can view with
any image viewer.


THE 4 VISUALIZATION TYPES
==========================

1. pitch_over_time.png - PITCH TIMELINE
   ------------------------------------
   Shows your performance pitch over time compared to the score.
   
   What you see:
   - BLUE BARS: Expected notes from the score
   - RED LINE: Your actual sung/played pitches
   - COLORED DOTS: Individual note measurements
     * Green dots = Perfect (within 10 cents)
     * Orange dots = Good (10-25 cents)
     * Dark orange = Acceptable (25-50 cents)  
     * Red dots = Poor (50+ cents off)
   
   Y-axis: Musical note names (C4, D4, E4, etc.) - easy to read!
   X-axis: Time in seconds
   
   What to look for:
   - Does the red line follow the blue bars closely?
   - Are there sections where you go flat (below) or sharp (above)?
   - Look for patterns - consistent drifting vs. random errors


2. note_errors.png - NOTE-BY-NOTE ACCURACY
   ----------------------------------------
   Bar chart showing each note's pitch error.
   
   What you see:
   - Each bar = one measured note
   - Bar color matches accuracy (green/orange/red)
   - POSITIVE bars = You were SHARP (too high)
   - NEGATIVE bars = You were FLAT (too low)
   - Horizontal reference lines at ±10¢, ±25¢, ±50¢
   
   X-axis: Note number and name
   Y-axis: Error in cents (0 = perfect)
   
   What to look for:
   - Which specific notes need work?
   - Are you consistently sharp or flat?
   - Are there particular notes that are harder for you?


3. accuracy_summary.png - OVERALL PERFORMANCE
   -------------------------------------------
   Visual summary with pie chart and key statistics.
   
   What you see:
   TOP: Pie chart breaking down your accuracy:
        - Green slice = Perfect notes (<10¢)
        - Orange = Good notes (10-25¢)
        - Dark orange = Acceptable (25-50¢)
        - Red = Poor notes (≥50¢)
   
   BOTTOM LEFT: Key statistics
        - Mean Error: Average cents off
        - Measurement Rate: % of notes detected in audio
        - Within ±10¢/25¢/50¢: Accuracy percentages
   
   BOTTOM RIGHT: Overall assessment
        - EXCELLENT, VERY GOOD, GOOD, FAIR, or NEEDS WORK
        - Based on your mean error
   
   What to look for:
   - What percentage of notes are in the "Perfect" category?
   - Is your mean error under 25 cents (good target)?


4. error_distribution.png - ERROR PATTERNS
   ----------------------------------------
   Histogram showing how your pitch errors are distributed.
   
   What you see:
   - Bars showing how many notes fall into each error range
   - Bar colors match accuracy levels (green/orange/red)
   - BLUE dashed line = Your median error
   - RED dashed line = Your mean error
   - Center line (0) = perfect pitch
   
   What to look for:
   - Is the histogram centered on 0? (Random errors - good!)
   - Is it shifted left (flat tendency) or right (sharp tendency)?
   - Wide spread = inconsistent, narrow = consistent but maybe biased
   
   INTERPRETATION TIPS:
   - Bell curve centered at 0 = Good random variation
   - Shifted curve = Systematic bias (always sharp/flat)
   - Flat distribution = Very inconsistent


HOW TO USE THESE VISUALIZATIONS
================================

FOR PRACTICE SESSIONS:
----------------------
1. Look at pitch_over_time.png first
   - Identify problem sections (where red line diverges from blue)
   - Note if you tend to go flat in long notes or sharp in runs

2. Check note_errors.png
   - Find which specific notes need work
   - Practice those problematic notes in isolation

3. Review error_distribution.png
   - If shifted consistently left/flat: work on support, vowel placement
   - If shifted consistently right/sharp: work on relaxation, jaw position
   - If very wide spread: work on consistency and listening skills


FOR PROGRESS TRACKING:
----------------------
1. Save these plots from each practice session
2. Compare accuracy_summary.png over time
   - Is the green slice getting bigger?
   - Is your mean error decreasing?

3. Compare pitch_over_time.png from different sessions
   - Are the red lines getting closer to blue bars?
   - Are there fewer red dots and more green dots?


UNDERSTANDING CENTS
===================
Musicians should know:
- 100 cents = 1 semitone (half step)
- 10 cents = Barely noticeable, professional level
- 25 cents = Noticeable but acceptable
- 50 cents = Half a semitone, clearly audible
- 100 cents = A full semitone off (e.g., C instead of C#)


WHAT DIFFERENT RESULTS MEAN
============================

EXCELLENT RESULTS (Mean Error < 10 cents):
- Professional/conservatory level accuracy
- Very tight pitch control
- Green dominates the pie chart
- Most dots on pitch_over_time are green

VERY GOOD RESULTS (Mean Error 10-25 cents):
- Strong pitch control
- Minor deviations only
- Mix of green and orange in pie chart
- Good foundation to build on

GOOD RESULTS (Mean Error 25-50 cents):
- Generally accurate with some noticeable drift
- Orange dominates pie chart
- Pitch is recognizable but needs refinement
- Focus on consistency

NEEDS WORK (Mean Error > 50 cents):
- Significant pitch issues
- Red appears prominently in charts
- May indicate:
  * Microphone/audio quality issues
  * Need for ear training
  * Technical vocal/instrumental issues
  * Wrong octave being played


LOW MEASUREMENT RATE (<50%)
----------------------------
If "Measurement Rate" in accuracy_summary.png is low:
- Audio quality may be poor (noise, distortion)
- You might be singing/playing too softly
- There may be long silences in the recording
- Microphone placement might be incorrect

This doesn't mean you're singing badly - it means the system
couldn't detect pitch in those sections!

    ./visualize_all_segments.sh

in the Data_Pitch/pitch_results_movement1/ directory.


QUESTIONS & INTERPRETATION
===========================

Q: Why do I see fewer dots than notes in the score?
A: The system only plots measured notes. Silent sections, very quiet
   notes, or noisy audio may not be detected.

Q: My pitch_over_time shows red line way off from blue bars everywhere?
A: This might indicate:
   - Wrong audio file paired with wrong score
   - Playing/singing in different octave
   - Significant tuning issues (check your instrument/voice)
   - Audio quality problems

Q: I see no visualizations in my evaluation folder?
A: Run the ./visualize_all_segments.sh script to generate them.
   Make sure you have matplotlib and seaborn installed.

Q: Can I share these images?
A: Yes! They're PNG files - use them in presentations, reports,
   or share with teachers/conductors.
