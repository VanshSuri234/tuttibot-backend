PITCH EVALUATION - QUICK REFERENCE GUIDE
================================================================================

WHAT IT DOES:
Compares musical score (XML file) with audio recording (WAV file) to measure
pitch accuracy note-by-note.


QUICK START:
python evaluate_pitch.py --score piece.xml --audio recording.wav


KEY METRICS TO CHECK (in order):

1. MEASUREMENT RATE
   Where: Look for "Measurement Rate:" in report
   What: Percentage of notes successfully detected in audio
   Good: > 80%
   Warning: < 50% (audio may not match score or has poor quality)

2. ACCURACY SCORE
   Where: Look for "Accuracy Score: X/100"
   What: Overall pitch accuracy (0-100 scale)
   Excellent: > 80
   Good: 60-80
   Fair: 40-60
   Poor: < 40

3. MEAN ABSOLUTE CENTS ERROR (MACE)
   Where: Look for "Mean Absolute Error: X cents"
   What: Average pitch deviation
   Reference: 100 cents = 1 semitone, 50 cents = quarter tone
   Excellent: < 10 cents
   Very Good: 10-25 cents
   Acceptable: 25-50 cents
   Problematic: > 50 cents

4. SYSTEMATIC BIAS
   Where: Look for "Bias Detected: YES/NO"
   What: Consistent tendency to play sharp (high) or flat (low)
   Detected when average error > 10 cents in one direction


FILES GENERATED:

Essential Files:
- EVALUATION_SUMMARY.txt - Complete technical report
- MUSICIAN_REPORT.txt - Simplified for performers
- note_by_note_comparison.csv - Spreadsheet with all notes

Detailed Files:
- pitch_evaluation.json - All metrics in JSON format
- score_pitches.json - Notes extracted from score
- audio_pitches.json - Frequencies detected from audio
- comparison_results.json - Complete comparison data


HOW TO READ CSV FILE:

Open note_by_note_comparison.csv in Excel/LibreOffice:

Columns:
- note_index: Note number (1, 2, 3...)
- note_name: Musical note (C4, A4, etc.)
- onset_time: When note starts (seconds)
- duration: How long note lasts (seconds)
- expected_frequency: What score says (Hz)
- measured_frequency: What was played (Hz)
- cents_error: Difference in cents
- measured: Was this note detected? (true/false)

Sort by "cents_error" to find worst notes.


INTERPRETING RESULTS:

Scenario 1: Low Measurement Rate (< 50%)
Problem: Most notes not detected
Likely Cause: Audio doesn't match score, or audio is silent/quiet
Action: Verify correct audio file, check volume

Scenario 2: High Error (MACE > 100 cents)
Problem: Notes are very wrong (more than 1 semitone off)
Likely Cause: Audio is transposed, instrument out of tune, wrong file
Action: Check if audio is in same key as score, verify tuning

Scenario 3: Systematic Bias (> 20 cents)
Problem: Consistently sharp or flat
Likely Cause: Instrument tuning, transposition issue
Action: Check tuning standard (A4 = 440 Hz?), verify transposition

Scenario 4: Good Measurement Rate but High Error
Problem: All notes detected but many are wrong
Interpretation: Performance captured but pitch accuracy needs work
Action: Review CSV file to identify problem sections


CENTS REFERENCE:

0 cents = Perfect (in tune)
5 cents = Just barely noticeable
10 cents = Clearly noticeable
25 cents = Quarter tone (significantly off)
50 cents = Half semitone (very noticeable)
100 cents = Full semitone (wrong note by one key)
200 cents = Whole tone (wrong note by two keys)


COMMON FREQUENCIES:

Middle C (C4) = 261.6 Hz
A4 (tuning reference) = 440.0 Hz
A5 (octave higher) = 880.0 Hz

Each semitone up multiplies frequency by 1.05946


TROUBLESHOOTING:

"Could not export since transcription did not complete successfully"
→ PDF to XML conversion failed (complex score, try simpler segment)

"Only X% of notes measured"
→ Audio quality issue or wrong audio file

"MACE > 1000 cents"
→ Likely wrong audio file for this score

"Bias detected: FLAT, Magnitude: 100 cents"
→ Audio is 1 semitone lower than score (transposition issue)
