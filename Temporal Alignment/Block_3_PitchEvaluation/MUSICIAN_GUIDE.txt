GUIDE FOR MUSICIANS - HOW TO READ YOUR PITCH EVALUATION RESULTS
================================================================================

Hello! This guide will help you understand your pitch evaluation results.
No technical background needed - everything is explained in plain language.


================================================================================
WHAT WAS DONE
================================================================================

Your performance was analyzed by comparing:
1. What the musical score says you should play (expected pitches)
2. What the recording captured you actually playing (measured pitches)

The system measured how close each note was to the written pitch.


================================================================================
WHERE TO START
================================================================================

You'll find several files in your results folder. Start here:

1. FIRST: Open "MUSICIAN_REPORT.txt"
   - Simple summary written specifically for musicians
   - Easy-to-understand explanation of results
   - No complex formulas or jargon

2. SECOND: Open "note_by_note_comparison.csv" in Excel or LibreOffice
   - Spreadsheet showing every single note
   - Which notes were good, which need work
   - Easy to sort and filter


================================================================================
UNDERSTANDING YOUR MUSICIAN REPORT
================================================================================

The report has several sections:

Section 1: WHAT WAS MEASURED
- How many notes in the score: Total number of notes to play
- How many notes measured: How many the system could detect in your recording
- Important: If less than 50% measured, there may be an audio problem

Section 2: PITCH ACCURACY RESULTS
- Accuracy Score (0-100): Like a test score, higher is better
- Average Pitch Error: How many "cents" off on average
  * Think of cents like millimeters for pitch
  * 100 cents = 1 key on piano (1 semitone)
  * 10 cents or less = very close (excellent!)
  * 50 cents = halfway between two keys (noticeable)

Section 3: DETAILED BREAKDOWN
- Shows what percentage of notes were:
  * Perfect (within 10 cents) - barely noticeable if off at all
  * Very Good (10-25 cents) - slight deviation
  * Acceptable (25-50 cents) - noticeable but not a full semitone
  * Problematic (more than 50 cents) - clearly wrong

Section 4: PITCH TENDENCY
- Tells you if you tend to play sharp (too high) or flat (too low)
- Helps identify if you need to adjust overall tuning


================================================================================
READING THE SPREADSHEET (CSV FILE)
================================================================================

Open "note_by_note_comparison.csv" - this is your detailed report card.

What each column means:

note_index: Just a count (1st note, 2nd note, 3rd note...)

note_name: The musical note (like C4, A4, E5)
- Number after letter is the octave
- C4 is "Middle C"
- Higher number = higher pitch

onset_time: When this note starts in the recording (in seconds)
- Helps you find the note in the audio
- Example: 15.3 means 15.3 seconds into the recording

expected_frequency: What pitch the score asked for (in Hz)
- Hz = vibrations per second
- Higher number = higher pitch

measured_frequency: What pitch was actually played (in Hz)

cents_error: THE KEY NUMBER - how far off the note was
- 0 cents = perfect!
- Positive (+) = played too sharp (high)
- Negative (-) = played too flat (low)
- Bigger number = more wrong

measured: Was this note detected?
- true = yes, we measured it
- false = no (note was silent or unclear in recording)


HOW TO USE THE SPREADSHEET:

Find Your Problem Notes:
1. Click the "cents_error" column header
2. Click "Sort" (usually an icon with arrows)
3. Sort by largest to smallest numbers
4. The top rows are your most problematic notes

Find Perfect Notes:
1. Sort "cents_error" by absolute value (closest to 0)
2. These notes show what you did well - keep it up!

Find Missing Notes:
1. Filter "measured" column to show "false"
2. These notes weren't detected - were they too quiet?


================================================================================
WHAT THE NUMBERS MEAN FOR YOUR PLAYING
================================================================================

Accuracy Score:
- 90-100: Excellent! Professional level accuracy
- 80-89: Very good! Strong pitch control
- 70-79: Good! Generally on target
- 60-69: Fair - room for improvement
- Below 60: Needs work on pitch accuracy

Average Pitch Error (cents):
- 0-10 cents: Outstanding! Nearly perfect intonation
- 10-25 cents: Very good! Minor deviations
- 25-50 cents: Acceptable - noticeable but not wrong notes
- 50-100 cents: Problematic - approaching wrong notes
- Over 100 cents: Major issues - more than a semitone off

Measurement Rate:
- Over 80%: Good! Most notes were clear in the recording
- 50-80%: Fair - some notes were unclear or too soft
- Under 50%: Problem - audio quality issue or wrong recording


================================================================================
COMMON SITUATIONS EXPLAINED
================================================================================

"I got a low score but I think I played it right!"

Possible reasons:
1. The audio file might not match this score
   → Check: Did you analyze the right recording?
   
2. Your instrument might be out of tune
   → Check: Is your instrument properly tuned to A=440 Hz?
   
3. The score might be in a different key
   → Check: Is the score transposed? (common for Bb/Eb instruments)


"My measurement rate is very low (under 50%)"

This usually means:
1. Audio file doesn't match this musical score
2. Recording is mostly silent or very quiet
3. Recording quality is poor (too much noise)

Action: Verify you're analyzing the correct audio file


"It says I play consistently sharp (or flat)"

This is called "systematic bias" and means:
- Sharp: You're playing a bit too high on all notes
- Flat: You're playing a bit too low on all notes

This is often easier to fix than random errors because:
- It might just be a tuning adjustment
- Your pitch control is consistent (good!)
- You just need to shift everything slightly


"Some notes are perfect, others are way off"

This is actually good news! It means:
- You CAN play accurately (the good notes prove it)
- Some notes or passages are just more difficult
- You know exactly which spots to practice

Action: Look at the CSV, find the worst notes, practice those measures


================================================================================
USING RESULTS TO IMPROVE
================================================================================

Step 1: Identify Patterns
- Are errors random or in specific sections?
- Are certain pitch ranges harder (high notes, low notes)?
- Are fast passages less accurate than slow ones?

Step 2: Prioritize Practice
- Focus on the measures with largest cents errors
- Use the "onset_time" to find them in the recording
- Practice these sections slowly with a tuner

Step 3: Track Progress
- Record again after practicing
- Run another evaluation
- Compare accuracy scores to see improvement
- Goal: Reduce average cents error over time


================================================================================
TECHNICAL TERMS SIMPLIFIED
================================================================================

Frequency (Hz): 
How many times per second something vibrates. Higher Hz = higher pitch.
Example: A4 = 440 Hz (the tuning note)

Cents: 
A way to measure small pitch differences.
- 100 cents = distance between two piano keys (1 semitone)
- 50 cents = halfway between two keys (very noticeable)
- 10 cents = small difference (just barely noticeable)
- Like measuring height in millimeters instead of inches

Semitone:
The distance between adjacent piano keys (100 cents).
Example: C to C# is one semitone.

Systematic Bias:
Consistent tendency to play sharp (high) or flat (low).
Like a scale that's always 2 pounds off - consistent error.

Measurement Rate:
Percentage of notes where pitch was detected in the recording.
Low rate suggests audio quality issues.

================================================================================
FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
================================================================================

Q: Is this system judging my musicality?
A: No! This only measures pitch accuracy (right notes vs. wrong notes).
   It doesn't evaluate rhythm, dynamics, expression, tone quality, or any
   other musical aspects. It's just one tool for one specific skill.

Q: Why are some notes missing (not measured)?
A: Could be several reasons:
   - Note was very quiet or short
   - Background noise covered it
   - Note was part of a chord (system works best with single melody)
   - Audio quality issue at that moment

Q: My accuracy score seems low but I thought I played well?
A: Remember this is measuring pitch precision to within cents (very precise).
   Even good performances may have small deviations. Context matters:
   - Professional recording: might aim for 90+
   - Student performance: 70+ is often very good
   - Beginner: 50-60 can be acceptable progress

Q: Can I improve my score?
A: Absolutely! Regular practice with awareness of pitch issues helps.
   Many musicians see scores improve by 10-20 points over a few weeks
   when they focus on problematic sections identified by the analysis.

Q: What if the audio file was recorded at a different pitch?
A: If your recording is in a different key or transposed, the system will
   show very large errors (100+ cents) because it's comparing different
   notes entirely. Make sure audio and score match.
